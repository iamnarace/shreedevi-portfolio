Korea to Nepal Money Transfers: The $50,000 Annual Rule & Required Documents

By Shreedevi Gyawali • Assistant Manager, Hana Bank HQ • Updated 2026

Sending earnings back home to Nepal is one of the most critical financial activities for expatriates, workers, and entrepreneurs living in South Korea. However, South Korea strictly regulates overseas capital outflows under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (외국환거래법).

1. The Non-Documentation Annual Cap: $50,000 USD

Under current South Korean banking guidelines, a foreign resident (holding a valid Alien Registration Card) can generally remit up to $50,000 USD (or equivalent in KRW) per calendar year without submitting extensive proof of funds, provided they have designated a single primary commercial bank for foreign exchange.

Important: The \$50,000 cap is aggregated across all remittance channels. If you remit via multiple fintech apps and commercial banks, the Bank of Korea and the National Tax Service (NTS) monitor cumulative totals.

2. What Happens When You Exceed $50,000?

You can legally send more than \$50,000 per year, but you must prove that the money was earned legitimately within South Korea. Required documentation includes:

  • Certificate of Earned Income (소득금액증명원): Issued via Hometax (국세청 홈택스) proving paid taxes on your earnings.
  • Withholding Tax Receipt (근로소득원천징수영수증): Issued by your Korean employer.
  • Employment Contract & Pay Stubs (급여명세서): To verify current active employment.

3. Choosing the Right Channel: API vs. SWIFT

Hana Bank and partner banks offer direct API-based transfers (such as Hana Easy-One Remittance). For routine transfers between ₩300,000 and ₩5,000,000, API channels offer instant settlement directly to commercial banks and digital wallets in Nepal at institutional FX rates with minimal delays.
