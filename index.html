Remittance · Checked October 2026
ORIS

Sending money home from Korea in 2026

Since January 2026, every bank and remittance app in Korea shares one record of your overseas transfers. Here is what that means when you send money to Nepal or anywhere else.

US$5,000per transfer, no documents needed
1 recordyour yearly total is counted across all banks and apps
Any providerthe "designated bank" rule is gone

1What changed in January 2026

Korea started the Overseas Remittance Integrated System (ORIS). Before, each bank, app or remittance company tracked your limit separately. Now they all see the same real-time total, so your yearly limit is one limit, not one per app.

The old rule that made you choose one "designated bank" for larger transfers has also been removed. You can use whichever bank or licensed app suits you.

2How much can you send without documents?

  • A single transfer of up to US$5,000 does not need supporting documents.
  • There is also a yearly no-document total, counted across every provider you use. For Korean nationals it is US$100,000.
  • For foreign residents, ask your bank what your personal yearly limit is before a large transfer. Your bank can see exactly how much you have already used.
Don't split transfers. Sending many US$5,000 transfers to stay under the limit is reported to the National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service. Talk to your bank instead.

3Sending more than the limit

You can still send more. You just need to show where the money came from and why you are sending it. Common documents:

  • Your Alien Registration Card and passport
  • Proof of income, such as payslips, your employment contract or a tax withholding certificate (근로소득원천징수영수증)
  • The reason for the transfer, for example family support or tuition

4Before you press send

  • The receiver's full name exactly as it appears on their ID and bank account
  • Their bank name, account number and SWIFT code (or the partner bank code for an app transfer)
  • Compare the exchange rate and fee, not just the fee
  • Keep your receipts. They help if you need to show your history later

Banking · Checked October 2026
₩1,000,000

New account? Your daily transfer limit, explained

Many foreigners open a Korean bank account and find they can only move a small amount each day. This is called a limit account (한도제한계좌), and it is there to stop accounts being used for phone scams.

₩1,000,000per day by app or internet banking
₩1,000,000per day at an ATM
₩3,000,000per day at the branch counter

1Why your account is limited

When you open an account without showing what it will be used for, the bank opens it as a limit account. It works normally for everyday use, but with a cap on how much you can send or withdraw each day.

2The limits went up in 2024

Older guides say ₩300,000 a day. Since 2 May 2024, the limits are ₩1,000,000 a day online, ₩1,000,000 at an ATM and ₩3,000,000 at the counter. The change applied automatically, including to accounts that already existed. You did not need to apply.

One exception: an account that was once used in a phone scam keeps the old, lower limits.

3How to remove the limit

Show the bank what the account is for. Bring your Alien Registration Card and the document that matches your purpose:

  • Salary account: employment certificate or contract, or a recent payslip
  • Business: business registration certificate
  • Student: certificate of enrolment, if your bank accepts it

Exact requirements differ between banks, so call or check your bank's app before you visit.

Never lend or sell your account. Letting someone else use your bank account or card is a crime in Korea, even if they promise money. It can also get you banned from opening accounts.

Trade · Based on UCP 600
L/C

Letters of Credit, without the fear

A Letter of Credit is a bank's promise to pay the seller, as long as the seller hands over the right documents on time. If you import or export between Korea and South Asia, this is how to keep it simple.

1The four parties

BuyerApplicantAsks their bank to open the L/C
Buyer's bankIssuing bankPromises to pay if documents comply
Seller's bankAdvising bankChecks the L/C is genuine and passes it on
SellerBeneficiaryShips the goods and presents documents

Banks deal only in documents, not goods. If the papers match the L/C, the bank pays. If they don't, payment can be delayed or refused.

2Two time limits to remember

21 daysafter shipment, the latest you can usually present documents (and always before the L/C expires)
5 banking daysthe most a bank has to examine your documents
UCP 600the international rules most L/Cs follow

3Mistakes that hold up payment

  • Shipping after the latest shipment date, or presenting documents too late
  • A goods description on the invoice that doesn't match the L/C
  • An invoice amount higher than the L/C allows
  • Missing signatures, endorsements or the wrong number of originals
  • Names or addresses that differ from the L/C

4Before you ship

  • Read the L/C as soon as it arrives and ask for changes early
  • Check every date: shipment, presentation and expiry
  • Prepare documents from the L/C wording, not from old templates
  • Ask your bank to check your documents before formal presentation
