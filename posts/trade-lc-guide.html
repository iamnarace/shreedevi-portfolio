Letters of Credit (L/C) Essentials for Nepal-Korea Cross-Border Traders

By Shreedevi Gyawali • Assistant Manager, Hana Bank HQ • Updated 2026

Trading physical goods between South Korea and Nepal requires robust financial safeguards. A Letter of Credit (L/C) guarantees that the seller receives payment once compliant shipping documents are presented to the issuing bank.

1. The Four Key Parties in Cross-Border L/C Operations

  • Applicant (Buyer / Importer): Requests the issuing bank to open the L/C.
  • Issuing Bank (개설은행): Undertakes the legal commitment to honor payments upon compliant presentation.
  • Advising / Nominated Bank (통지은행): Informs the seller and receives shipping documents.
  • Beneficiary (Seller / Exporter): Dispatches cargo and presents stipulated documents.

2. Common Documentation Discrepancies to Avoid

Under the ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600), banks deal strictly in documents, not goods. Even slight discrepancies can lead to payment refusals:

  • Late shipment or late presentation beyond the specified expiry window.
  • Inconsistent descriptions of merchandise between commercial invoice and L/C text.
  • Missing endorsements on negotiable Bills of Lading (B/L).
Expert Insight: Always perform a pre-check of draft L/C wording before issuance to ensure operational alignment between your freight forwarder and commercial banking desks.
